What happened

India

The Nifty 50 closed at 25,607.20 points, down 0.82% on the day, while the Sensex fell 0.71% to 83,339.26 points. Banking stocks led the decline, with Nifty Bank at 63,040.90 points, down 1.04%, and the Financials sector slipping 1.21% to 30,279.70 points. Auto lagged further, down 2.16% to 28,199.90 points, whereas Pharma was flat and FMCG dropped 1.86% to 49,212.45 points. Metal rallied 2.59% to 11,878.00 points, Energy rose 0.72% to 36,935.00 points, and Realty gained 1.80% to 958.95 points. The India VIX eased 6.37% to 16.55, reflecting a calmer market after a four‑week rally snapped amid concerns over higher crude and SEBI’s possible IPO retail‑limit review.

IT stocks provided the only bright spot, with Nifty IT climbing 1.71% to 32,758.10 points, buoyed by earnings beats and continued demand for digital services. The sector’s strength helped offset the banking weakness and contributed to a modest breadth improvement despite overall negative sentiment. Metal’s 2.59% rise and Energy’s 0.72% gain suggest a rotation toward commodities and infrastructure‑linked names, while Realty’s 1.80% advance hints at renewed confidence in the property market. The RBI governor’s call for MSMEs to adopt AI may underpin future demand for tech solutions, reinforcing the IT rally. Meanwhile, the HEG demerger news added a brief flare of volatility, but the broader market remained defensive.

Volatility contracted as the India VIX fell to 16.55, yet market breadth remained thin, with most sectors in the red. The rupee weakened to 92.98 per USD, up 0.59%, adding import‑cost pressure, especially as Brent crude rose 2% to $92.45 a barrel. Gold’s 3.77% jump to $3,698.90 per ounce further signalled risk aversion, while Bitcoin’s 1.89% gain to $61,636.98 had limited spill‑over. Investors are cautioned against panic‑selling, echoing advice that new SIP investors should resist the urge to exit. The combination of higher commodity prices and a softer rupee could keep inflationary pressures alive, weighing on cost‑sensitive sectors like auto and FMCG, which posted the steepest declines.

Looking ahead, Indian markets will open under the shadow of mixed global cues. US equities closed mixed – the S&P 500 down 0.25% to 7,182.78 points, Nasdaq down 0.62% to 23,585.80 points, while the Dow rose 0.33% to 50,182.95 points – and US 10‑year yields slipped to 4.85%, a modest easing. European sentiment is positive, with the FTSE 100 up 0.71% to 10,684.15 points, and Asian benchmarks surged, the Nikkei 225 gaining 2.11% to 58,711.11 points and Hang Seng climbing 3.32% to 22,694.77 points. SEBI’s plan to cut FPI onboarding time could attract fresh foreign capital, but the rupee’s weakness and higher oil prices may keep the opening cautious.

Global markets

US markets closed with a mixed picture: the S&P 500 slipped 0.25% to 7,182.78 points, the Nasdaq fell 0.62% to 23,585.80 points, while the Dow Jones rose 0.33% to 50,182.95 points. The US 10‑year Treasury yield eased 1.14% to 4.85%, indicating a slight reduction in borrowing costs. This modest easing may temper the outflow of foreign funds from emerging markets, but the equity weakness, especially in growth‑oriented tech stocks, could limit appetite for Indian equities at the open.

European equities showed resilience, with the FTSE 100 up 0.71% to 10,684.15 points, suggesting a risk‑on bias in the region. In Asia, the Nikkei 225 surged 2.11% to 58,711.11 points and the Hang Seng jumped 3.32% to 22,694.77 points, reflecting strong regional risk appetite. These gains could provide a counterbalance to US weakness, offering a supportive backdrop for Indian markets, particularly for export‑oriented and technology‑heavy stocks that track global sentiment.

Commodities added another layer of complexity: Brent crude rose 2% to $92.45 a barrel, while gold climbed 3.77% to $3,698.90 per ounce, and Bitcoin advanced 1.89% to $61,636.98. Higher oil prices raise the import bill for India, pressuring the current account and the rupee, whereas gold’s rally underscores a flight to safety that can siphon capital from equities. Crypto’s modest rise has limited direct impact on Indian investors but signals broader risk‑on dynamics that could spill over into market sentiment.

Rupee, rates and commodities

The rupee weakened to 92.98 per USD, up 0.59%, reflecting a modest depreciation against the dollar. US 10‑year yields fell to 4.85%, easing by 1.14%, which could support equity valuations but may not offset the rupee’s slide. Brent crude’s 2% rise to $92.45 a barrel and gold’s 3.77% jump to $3,698.90 per ounce add inflationary pressure, especially on oil‑importing economies like India. Bitcoin’s 1.89% increase to $61,636.98 is largely a peripheral factor. Together, these macro forces suggest higher import costs and a weaker currency, which could dampen corporate earnings and keep inflation concerns alive.

Higher crude prices directly affect India’s oil‑import bill, squeezing margins for energy‑intensive sectors such as auto and FMCG, both of which posted notable declines today. Conversely, the surge in metal prices, reflected in a 2.59% rise in the Metal index, may benefit mining and related exporters, offering a partial offset. Gold’s rally supports domestic jewellery demand, a bright spot for consumer‑discretionary spending, but also signals broader risk aversion that can pull funds away from equities. Bitcoin’s modest gain remains a niche influence, unlikely to shift mainstream investment flows.

Going forward, the trajectory of US yields and the Fed’s stance will be pivotal. If yields stay low and the Fed signals a pause, the rupee could stabilise, easing import‑cost pressures. However, continued Brent strength may keep the rupee under pressure, sustaining volatility despite the India VIX’s recent dip to 16.55. Market participants should watch for any surprise in US inflation data that could reignite rate‑hike expectations, as that would likely re‑energise the dollar and further weaken the rupee, setting a cautious tone for tomorrow’s Indian market open.
