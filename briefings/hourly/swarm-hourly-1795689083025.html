Indian equities staged a sharp recovery, with the Nifty 50 up 2.11% and Nifty IT surging 4.82% on easing global tariff fears. The rally was supported by a 4.89% drop in India VIX and a modest rupee weakness, while global cues remained mixed with US markets closed for the holiday.

What happened

India

The Indian market closed firmly in positive territory on Thursday, with the Nifty 50 gaining 2.11% to settle at 25,884.50. The Sensex mirrored this strength, rising 2.30% to 84,328.59, while the Nifty Bank index added 1.52% to reach 63,535.30. The standout performer was the Nifty IT index, which surged 4.82% to 33,386.95, reflecting a significant rotation into technology stocks. This broad-based rally suggests a recovery in investor sentiment after recent volatility, with the IT sector leading the charge as global tech concerns eased.

Sectoral breadth was mixed but leaned positive, with Realty leading the gains at 3.29% and Pharma and FMCG both up nearly 2.8%. In contrast, Metal stocks fell 0.89% and PSU Bank stocks slipped 0.31%, indicating that the rally was not uniform across all segments. The Financials index remained nearly flat at -0.10%, suggesting that while banks contributed to the overall index gain, the broader financial sector was cautious. This divergence highlights that the market is still selective, with investors favoring defensive and growth-oriented sectors over cyclical heavyweights.

Volatility measures pointed to a significant de-escalation in market stress, with the India VIX dropping 4.89% to 16.84. This decline in the fear gauge indicates that investors are becoming more comfortable with the current market direction, reducing the premium for downside protection. The drop in VIX often precedes sustained rallies, as it suggests that hedging activity is winding down and long positions are being built with greater confidence. This shift in volatility dynamics supports the view that the recent rally has a solid foundation rather than being a short-lived relief bounce.

The primary driver of the day's move appears to be a combination of easing global tariff fears and strong domestic earnings. Reports that Trump's proposed tariffs for India are less severe than for rival nations likely provided a relief rally for export-oriented sectors like IT. Additionally, the strong performance of IT stocks suggests that investors are betting on a recovery in global tech spending. The market's ability to absorb these global cues and translate them into a broad-based rally indicates a resilient domestic demand base, which is crucial for sustaining the upward momentum in the coming weeks.

Global markets

US markets were closed for the Thanksgiving holiday, leaving the most recent data from the Wednesday session where the S&P 500 fell 0.25% to 7,182.78 and the Nasdaq dropped 0.62% to 23,585.80. The Dow Jones, however, managed a 0.33% gain to 50,182.95, indicating a rotation into value stocks. The absence of fresh US data means Indian markets are trading on the last known US sentiment, which was slightly negative for tech but positive for industrials. This mixed signal from the US could lead to some uncertainty in the Indian IT sector, although the strong domestic rally suggests that local factors are currently dominating.

European markets provided a positive cue, with the FTSE 100 rising 1.51% to 10,584.47 as tech and banks rebounded and tariff fears eased. This rally in Europe is significant for India because it indicates a broader global risk-on sentiment, which is supportive for emerging market equities. The strength in European banks also suggests that global credit conditions are not tightening, which is a positive for Indian financials. The alignment of European and Indian market moves suggests that global liquidity is improving, which could lead to further FII inflows into Indian markets in the near term.

Asian markets were mixed, with the Nikkei 225 gaining 0.86% to 57,499.16 and the Hang Seng index nearly flat at 22,226.88. The strength in the Nikkei is notable, as it often leads the Asian tech rally, which could be a positive signal for Indian IT stocks. The flat Hang Seng suggests that Chinese demand remains weak, which is a concern for commodity exporters. The mixed Asian cues indicate that the global recovery is not uniform, and Indian markets will need to navigate these divergent signals. The overall global picture is one of cautious optimism, with risk-on sentiment in Europe and Japan but caution in the US and China.

Rupee, rates and commodities

The rupee weakened slightly against the dollar, with USD/INR rising 0.24% to 92.44. This modest depreciation is a concern for importers and could lead to higher inflationary pressures, but it is not severe enough to derail the equity rally. The rupee's stability is supported by strong FII inflows and a robust current account surplus. However, investors should monitor the rupee's movement closely, as a sharp depreciation could lead to a correction in the market. The current level of 92.44 is within the expected range, and no immediate action is required from the RBI.

US 10-year yields fell 1.14% to 4.85% in the Wednesday session, which is a positive for global growth and emerging market equities. Lower yields reduce the cost of borrowing and support asset prices, which is beneficial for Indian markets. The decline in yields also suggests that the Fed is not in a hurry to hike rates, which is a supportive factor for global liquidity. This improvement in the global rate environment is a key driver of the recent rally in Indian equities, and it is likely to continue supporting the market in the coming weeks.

Crude oil prices fell 0.35% to $90.32 per barrel, which is a positive for India as a net importer of oil. Lower oil prices reduce the import bill and improve the current account deficit, which is supportive for the rupee and the economy. Gold prices rose 0.72% to $3,590.10, reflecting safe-haven demand, but the domestic MCX gold price is still well below its record high. Bitcoin gained 0.61% to $60,748.80, indicating continued interest in alternative assets. The mixed commodity and crypto signals suggest that investors are diversifying their portfolios, which is a healthy sign for the overall market.

The Nifty 50 closed at 25,884.50, up 2.11%, while the Sensex finished at 84,328.59, a 2.30% gain. The rally was anchored by a 4.82% surge in the Nifty IT index and a 1.52% rise in Nifty Bank, reflecting strong earnings from technology exporters and renewed confidence in the banking sector. Domestic consumption data released earlier in the day showed a modest uptick in retail sales, adding to the risk‑on tone. Meanwhile, the India VIX slipped 4.89% to 16.84, signalling a low‑volatility environment that typically underpins broad equity buying.

On the macro front, the rupee traded at 92.44 per dollar, a 0.24% weakening that reflects lingering concerns over potential US tariff measures on Indian exports. Commodity markets were largely benign: Brent crude fell 0.35% to $90.32, easing input costs for energy‑intensive firms, while gold rose 0.72% to $3,590, offering a safe‑haven buffer. Global equity cues were mixed – the S&P 500 and Nasdaq slipped, but the Dow Jones and FTSE 100 posted gains – leaving overall risk appetite intact but cautious.

What would change our view

A hardening of US‑India tariff negotiations could hit export‑linked equities, especially IT and pharma, eroding the earnings momentum that underpins today’s rally. Any escalation would also pressure the rupee further, amplifying import‑cost concerns across sectors.

If the rupee slides beyond the 93 per dollar threshold, import‑dependent industries such as metal and energy could see margin compression, while foreign portfolio inflows may retreat, prompting a spike in the India VIX and a broader market correction.

A sudden rise in US Treasury yields, particularly the 10‑year benchmark, could trigger a risk‑off wave in global markets, lifting the VIX and prompting investors to rotate out of Indian equities. Such a shift would test the resilience of the current low‑volatility environment.
