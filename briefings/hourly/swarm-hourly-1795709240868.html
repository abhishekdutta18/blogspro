What happened

India

The Nifty 50 closed at 25,884.50 rupees, up 2.11% on the day, while the Sensex rose 2.30% to 84,328.59 rupees. Breadth was broad, with the Nifty Bank gaining 1.52% to 63,535.30 rupees and the Nifty IT posting a 4.82% jump to 33,386.95 rupees. The India VIX fell 4.89% to 16.84, signalling a retreat in market nervousness after a week of strong gains. The rally capped a November in which the Nifty has already logged a 12.3% gain, positioning it for its best monthly performance since April 2020.

Sectoral performance was mixed but tilted positive. Financials slipped 0.10% to 30,649.45 rupees and PSU banks fell 0.31% to 9,529.90 rupees, reflecting lingering concerns over credit quality. In contrast, autos edged up 0.47% to 28,623.45 rupees, pharma surged 2.84% to 29,090.85 rupees, and FMCG rose 2.82% to 49,673.55 rupees, buoyed by consumer‑spending optimism. Metals lagged 0.89% at 11,713.50 rupees, while energy ticked higher 0.61% to 36,880.30 rupees on the back of oil price movements. Realty posted the strongest gain, up 3.29% to 972.85 rupees, driven by renewed developer confidence.

Volatility eased as the India VIX dropped 4.89%, reflecting reduced fear after a series of strong earnings and foreign inflows. The IT sector’s 4.82% surge was anchored by expectations of higher software exports and a rebound in global tech spending. Pharma and FMCG benefited from domestic demand data and a softer rupee that makes imports costlier, prompting consumers to shift to locally produced goods. The modest rise in energy helped offset the metal weakness, while the real estate rally suggested confidence in the longer‑term growth story. Overall, the market’s upward thrust was underpinned by a combination of sectoral catalysts and a more benign risk environment.

Looking ahead to Friday’s open, the Indian market is likely to start higher. Europe’s FTSE 100 is trading up 1.95%, and Asian indices are modestly positive, providing a supportive backdrop. Although the rupee weakened to 92.44 per dollar, the dollar’s recent softness and a retreat in US Treasury yields should limit further pressure on equities. Domestic investors will watch the Nifty IT and pharma moves closely, while the narrowing VIX suggests that the rally can sustain momentum. Barring any surprise on the Fed’s December meeting, the confluence of global risk‑on sentiment and strong sectoral fundamentals points to a bullish opening bell.

What would change our view

A sustained spike in Brent crude beyond the live 91.64 USD level would validate the risk manager's caution and could force Indian bonds lower, triggering a sharper retreat in risk assets than the current constructive stance anticipates. If the rupee continues to weaken against the dollar, with USD/INR rising further above 92.44 INR, import-heavy sectors and foreign investor appetite would face renewed pressure, undermining the momentum strategist's bullish case. Should US 10-year yields reverse their recent slide and climb back toward prior highs, the macro economist's thesis of easing financing costs for Indian corporates would fracture, potentially dragging the Nifty 50 away from its 25884.50 INR close.
