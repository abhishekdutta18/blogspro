What happened

India

The Nifty 50 closed at 25884.50 points, up 2.11% from the previous close on Thursday, while the Sensex gained 2.30% to finish at 84328.59 points. The Nifty Bank added 1.52% to reach 63535.30 points, and the Nifty IT index jumped 4.82% to 33386.95 points, reflecting strong risk appetite in technology names. The India VIX settled at 16.84 points, down 4.89%, indicating a significant drop in expected equity volatility heading into the next session. Sectoral breadth was mixed, with Pharma rising 2.84% to 29090.85 points and FMCG gaining 2.82% to 49673.55 points, while Financials slipped 0.10% to 30649.45 points. Auto edged up 0.47% to 28623.45 points, Energy advanced 0.61% to 36880.30 points, and Realty surged 3.29% to 972.85 points. In contrast, Metal declined 0.89% to 11713.50 points and PSU Bank fell 0.31% to 9529.90 points, showing selective profit taking among public sector lenders and commodity producers after the recent run up.

Global markets

The S&P 500 closed at 7182.78 points, down 0.25% on Wednesday, while the Nasdaq slipped 0.62% to 23585.80 points. The Dow Jones managed a modest 0.33% gain to 50182.95 points. In Europe, the FTSE 100 is live and trading at 10630.02 points, reflecting firmer sentiment on the continent. Asian markets closed earlier, with the Nikkei 225 finishing at 57499.16 points, up 0.86% on Thursday, and the Hang Seng closing at 22226.88 points, barely changed at 0.03%. These divergent closes suggest that European investors are pricing in a different risk calculus than their US counterparts, which will influence the opening tone for Indian exchanges tomorrow. The recovery in Japan and the flat performance in Hong Kong provide a cautiously optimistic backdrop, though the weakness in US tech weighs on global growth expectations.

Rupee, rates and commodities

The USD/INR closed at 92.44 INR per USD, up 0.23%, meaning the rupee weakened against the dollar on Thursday. The US 10Y yield settled at 4.85% yield, down 1.14% on Wednesday, signalling a relief rally in long duration paper. Brent crude is live at 91.64 USD per barrel, up 1.10%, keeping energy import costs elevated. Gold is live at 3597.10 USD per troy ounce, up 0.91%, supported by safe haven demand despite the yield decline. Bitcoin is live at 60549.49 USD, up 0.28%, showing muted crypto appetite. The combination of a softer rupee, higher oil, and elevated gold points to continued pressure on the current account and import bill, while the drop in yields offers some relief to domestic bond valuations ahead of the Friday opening.
