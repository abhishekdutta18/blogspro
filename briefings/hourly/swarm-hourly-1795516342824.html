What happened

India

The Nifty 50 finished at 25,349.65, down 1.75% from the previous close, while the Sensex slipped to 82,435.99, a 1.77% decline. Bank Nifty led the rout, falling 3.17% to 62,585.35, whereas the Nifty IT index rose 1.69% to 31,850.80. The sell‑off was anchored in a sharp correction after a month‑long rally that saw foreign inflows exceed Rs 1 lakh crore, prompting profit‑taking across the board. RBI Deputy Governor Rohit Jain’s warning to primary dealers about volatile G‑Sec pricing added to the nervousness, especially in the banking segment where liquidity concerns lingered.

Sector breadth was decidedly negative. Financials dropped 2.31% to 30,017.30, with PSU banks falling 3.25% to 9,559.65, reflecting heightened sensitivity to the RBI’s call for disciplined bidding. The metal index plunged 3.68% to 11,819.25, while energy slipped 1.78% to 36,655.10. Auto and FMCG were modestly down 1.03% and 0.46% respectively, and pharma edged lower by 0.35%. Realty was the lone sector in positive territory, up 0.31% to 941.85, but its impact on the broader market was negligible. The overall picture was a broad-based retreat, with only IT managing to defy the trend.

India VIX rose 2.18% to 17.70, signalling heightened market anxiety after the sharp equity decline. The jump in implied volatility mirrored the rapid unwind of short‑term positions, especially in banking and metal stocks, and reflected concerns over the RBI’s tightening stance on government‑bond underwriting. Traders cited the combination of profit‑booking, the RBI’s warning, and the looming US Thanksgiving holiday as catalysts for the heightened fear gauge. The elevated VIX suggests that further downside moves remain possible if sentiment does not stabilize.

The market’s direction was shaped by a mix of domestic and policy factors. RBI DG Rohit Jain’s admonition to primary dealers to bid responsibly in G‑Sec auctions raised doubts about future liquidity, pressuring banks and financials. At the same time, the announcement of a partial divestment in IIFCL through an IPO added a layer of uncertainty about capital allocation in infrastructure financing. Meanwhile, defence‑sector optimism, highlighted by Choice Institutional Equities’ buy recommendations for several defence stocks, provided a modest lift to the IT index, which often tracks broader risk‑on sentiment. Overall, the confluence of policy caution and profit‑taking drove the market lower.

The US equity markets closed sharply higher on Monday, with the S&P 500 up 4.73% to 7,237.17, the Nasdaq surging 6.51% to 24,089.56, and the Dow Jones gaining 3.01% to 49,595.31. The rally was powered by a late‑week Nvidia bounce and strong earnings, but it came just before the Thanksgiving holiday, which will pause US trading for two days. The surge attracted foreign portfolio inflows, yet the imminent market closure may delay the flow of fresh capital into Indian equities, potentially dampening the short‑term rebound that some investors hoped for after the recent sell‑off.

Global markets

The US equity surge, highlighted by a 6.51% jump in the Nasdaq, underscores a risk‑on bias that could spill over into emerging markets once US trading resumes. However, the upcoming Thanksgiving holiday creates a two‑day gap, potentially delaying any immediate capital inflows into India. Investors will be watching the post‑holiday US open for clues on whether the rally sustains, as a reversal could quickly reverse the temporary optimism that lifted global risk assets.

European markets offered little in the way of directional cues, with the FTSE 100 barely moving. The continent’s focus on domestic inflation data and the anticipation of a quiet US week meant that European investors remained on the sidelines. For Indian markets, this translates to limited European fund flow pressure, keeping the primary driver of short‑term capital movements firmly in the US‑India corridor.

Asian markets presented a split picture: Japan’s strong gains contrast with Hong Kong’s modest decline. The Nikkei’s 3.91% rise reflects confidence in export‑driven growth, while the Hang Seng’s 0.85% fall signals lingering concerns over China’s economic slowdown. This divergence suggests that Indian investors may see selective Asian inflows, with a bias toward sectors that benefit from Japan’s tech rally, but overall risk appetite remains tempered by regional uncertainties.

Rupee, rates and commodities

The rupee closed unchanged at 92.90 per US dollar, indicating a stable currency despite a 0.12% rise in the US 10‑year yield to 4.92%. RBI’s interventions and the recent influx of foreign capital helped anchor the rupee, offsetting the upward pressure from higher US yields. Stability in the exchange rate supports import‑dependent sectors and keeps external debt servicing costs predictable for Indian corporates.

US Treasury yields climbed 0.12% to 4.92%, nudging Indian government bond yields higher and raising the cost of borrowing for both the sovereign and corporate sectors. At the same time, Brent crude edged up 0.10% to $86.77, keeping import‑linked inflation in check but still posing a risk if oil prices accelerate. The combination of higher global yields and modest oil price pressure could tighten domestic liquidity, especially for banks already under stress from the RBI’s G‑Sec concerns.

Gold slipped 0.79% to $3,622.50, while Bitcoin fell 2.29% to $59,810.28, reflecting a shift away from safe‑haven assets amid the equity sell‑off. The decline in gold reduces the attractiveness of hedging against rupee volatility, while the crypto dip may deter speculative capital from flowing into Indian markets. Both moves reinforce a risk‑off sentiment that aligns with the higher India VIX reading and the broader equity weakness observed on Monday.
