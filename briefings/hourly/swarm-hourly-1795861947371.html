What happened

India

The Nifty 50 closed at 25,607.20 points, down 1.07%, and the Sensex fell to 83,339.26 points, off 1.17% in the Friday session. Bank Nifty slipped 0.78% to 63,040.90, while the Nifty IT lagged further, down 1.88% at 32,758.10. Breadth was thin: Financials dropped 1.21% to 30,279.70, PSU Bank slipped 0.40% to 9,491.80, Auto fell 1.48% to 28,199.90, Pharma down 0.40% to 28,973.55, and FMCG lost 0.93% to 49,212.45. Metal rose 1.40% to 11,878.00 and Energy edged up 0.15% to 36,935.00, while Realty declined 1.43% to 958.95. The India VIX eased 1.75% to 16.55, reflecting reduced intraday panic but not enough to reverse the sell‑off.

Sector dynamics were shaped by external cues. A rare midday pledge from Chinese authorities sparked a rally in Asian markets, yet the domestic impact was muted as investors focused on domestic cost pressures. The metal sector’s 1.40% gain mirrored a copper surge that hit its strongest level since April, feeding optimism in miners but not enough to lift the broader market. Energy’s modest 0.15% rise tracked Brent’s 0.22% increase to $90.84 per barrel, keeping import‑dependent stocks under pressure. Meanwhile, the IT index’s 1.88% decline highlighted lingering concerns over global tech valuations after a recent AI‑driven sell‑off.

Volatility receded, with the India VIX down 1.75% to 16.55, the lowest level since early October, suggesting that panic was fading even as prices slipped. Fund managers warned of deeper corrections, citing rising crude oil prices and a weakening rupee as headwinds for corporate earnings. The rupee’s 0.60% rise to 92.99 per dollar signalled a weaker currency, adding import cost stress. Despite the subdued VIX, market breadth remained narrow, and the NSE CEO’s recent comments on growing household participation did little to offset the prevailing risk‑off tone.

Looking ahead to Monday, investors will watch the rupee’s trajectory, US Treasury yields and crude oil levels for clues on momentum. A further weakening of the rupee beyond 93.00 could pressure import‑heavy stocks, while a breakout in US 10‑year yields above 5% would likely lift domestic bond yields and compress equity valuations. Commodity‑linked sectors such as metals and energy may find support if copper and oil continue their modest rebounds, but any resurgence in global risk appetite will need to overcome the lingering caution evident in today’s breadth and volatility metrics.

Global markets

US equity markets posted solid gains on Friday, with the S&P 500 up 1.60% to 7,297.49, the Nasdaq climbing 2.31% to 24,129.50, and the Dow Jones rising 1.18% to 50,774.94. The rally was driven by better‑than‑expected earnings and a perception that the Federal Reserve may be nearing the end of its rate‑hiking cycle, which helped lift risk appetite globally. For Indian investors, the US upside provided a modest tailwind for foreign inflows, but the impact was tempered by a stronger dollar that kept the rupee under pressure.

European and Asian markets also posted gains, with the FTSE 100 up 0.84% to 10,698.81, the Nikkei 225 advancing 2.11% to 58,711.11, and Hong Kong’s Hang Seng rising 2.10% to 22,694.77. A rare midday statement from Chinese regulators spurred the biggest single‑day rally in the region since 2024, lifting sentiment in the broader Asia‑Pacific corridor. The copper surge, the most pronounced in seven months, reflected renewed risk appetite and fed into the metal sector’s modest rise in India, while the broader Asian optimism offered limited offset to domestic headwinds.

Collectively, the global equity bounce underscored a tentative shift from defensive positioning to selective risk‑taking. However, the gains were uneven, and the lingering concerns over crude oil price hikes and a resilient US Treasury yield curve meant that Indian markets remained vulnerable to external shocks. The mixed global backdrop suggests that any sustained inflow into Indian equities will depend on domestic catalysts, such as corporate earnings resilience and policy support, rather than pure spill‑over from overseas rallies.

Rupee, rates and commodities

The rupee closed at 92.99 per dollar, a 0.60% depreciation that signalled a weaker currency and added pressure on import‑dependent firms. US 10‑year Treasury yields rose 1.82% to 4.94%, pushing up global borrowing costs and hinting at tighter financial conditions. Brent crude edged 0.22% higher to $90.84 per barrel, reinforcing the rupee’s downside and raising cost concerns for energy‑intensive sectors. Gold rallied 2.91% to $3,668.50 per ounce, reflecting safe‑haven demand amid the same risk‑off sentiment that dampened Indian equities. Together, these macro variables created a backdrop of higher input costs and tighter financing that weighed on market sentiment.

Gold’s near‑3% jump reinforced its role as a low‑volatility store of value, a point highlighted by the World Gold Council’s observation that gold has exhibited the lowest daily volatility among major asset classes since 2005. Meanwhile, Bitcoin, trading live at $59,273.35, slipped 0.99%, indicating that crypto remained a peripheral factor for Indian investors focused on equity and commodity dynamics. The divergence between gold’s strength and crypto’s weakness underscored a broader shift toward traditional safe‑haven assets as investors navigated the twin pressures of a weakening rupee and rising global yields.

The transmission of these macro trends to Indian assets was evident across the board. A weaker rupee amplified the impact of higher oil prices on balance sheets, especially for airlines and logistics firms, while elevated US yields pressured domestic bond markets, nudging equity valuations lower. Gold’s rally offered a modest hedge for portfolio managers, but the overall risk‑off tone limited its offsetting effect. Crypto’s muted performance had negligible spill‑over, reinforcing the view that Indian investors remain anchored to fundamentals rather than speculative digital assets as they brace for the next trading session.

In India, the metal index posted a 1.40% increase, the only sector to close in positive territory, indicating that copper’s upward momentum is feeding into domestic metal stocks. The surge may also be linked to the Chinese market boost described in headline 1, as copper is a key input for Chinese manufacturing. However, the headline offers no detail on the drivers of the price move—whether it is supply constraints, demand forecasts, or speculative positioning—so the durability of the rally remains uncertain. A slowdown in Chinese industrial activity or a reversal in global risk sentiment could quickly erode copper’s gains, pressuring metal‑heavy portfolios.
