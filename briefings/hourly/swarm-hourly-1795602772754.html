What happened

India

The Nifty 50 closed at 25,819.25, up 1.85% on the day, while the Sensex finished at 83,938.28, a 1.82% rise. Bank Nifty added 1.79% to 63,703.25 and the Nifty IT index climbed 1.12% to 32,207.55. Market participants cited a bounce‑back narrative after a weak start to the week, with the Moneycontrol headline noting a 1,502‑point lift in the Sensex and the Nifty breaking the 25,800 barrier. Broad buying across large‑cap and mid‑cap stocks reinforced the rally, and the live data showed the market trading with a clear upward bias as investors digested the latest earnings and macro cues.

Sector breadth was robust, led by Financials, which jumped 2.21% to 30,681.50, and FMCG, the top performer, rising 3.79% to 50,143.50. Pharma added 2.38% and Auto gained 1.16%, indicating diversified buying beyond the core banking space. By contrast, Metal lagged sharply, slipping 2.04% to 11,578.50 amid supply‑concern headlines, while Energy was flat at 36,669.20 and Realty barely moved. The PSU Bank index rose modestly 0.37%, suggesting that even state‑run lenders benefited from the overall risk‑on sentiment, while the narrow decline in metals tempered the broader optimism.

India VIX held steady at 17.70, unchanged from the previous close, signalling that the market’s risk appetite remained intact despite the sharp equity gains. The flat volatility reading reflects a consensus that the rally is driven by fundamentals rather than speculative excess. Traders appear comfortable with the current price levels, as the VIX has not spiked to signal panic. This stability in implied volatility, combined with the breadth of sector participation, suggests that the market is not overly jittery and is likely to stay in a measured advance unless new macro shocks emerge.

The rally was underpinned by a combination of a stronger rupee, softer commodity prices and a supportive global backdrop. USD/INR fell 0.73% to 92.22, giving the rupee an eight‑month high and reducing the cost of imported inputs for corporates, which buoyed earnings expectations. Brent crude slipped 0.30% to $88.51, easing inflation pressures, while gold fell 0.82% to $3,561.60, further confirming a risk‑on tone. These macro inputs, together with positive domestic earnings narratives such as the wealth‑manager upgrade potential for 360 One, helped set the stage for a continued upward bias into Friday’s session.

Global markets

U.S. equity markets closed mixed on Tuesday, with the S&P 500 down 0.50% to 7,201.09 and the Nasdaq slipping 1.48% to 23,733.53, while the Dow Jones rose 0.85% to 50,018.70. The divergence reflected a cautious optimism in industrials offset by tech weakness, a pattern that filtered into Indian sentiment as investors weighed the impact of weaker tech earnings on global growth forecasts. The modest decline in the S&P and Nasdaq reinforced the view that risk appetite is selective, but the Dow’s gain offered a counterbalance that helped sustain the Indian rally.

In Europe, the FTSE 100 is trading live up 0.38% at 10,506.57, indicating that the UK market is holding its ground despite broader global uncertainty. The modest gain in the FTSE reflects a relatively resilient consumer sector and a supportive monetary stance from the Bank of England, which has helped keep funding costs stable. This European steadiness contributed to a risk‑on environment that spilled over into Asian markets, reinforcing the positive bias seen in Indian equities.

Asian markets were largely negative, with the Nikkei 225 closing down 2.78% at 57,011.62 and Hong Kong’s Hang Seng falling 1.87% to 21,966.01. The steep slide in Japan’s benchmark was driven by weaker export data and concerns over a slowing domestic economy, while Hang Seng’s decline reflected regional trade tensions and a softer Chinese property outlook. These Asian drags tempered the global risk appetite but were insufficient to derail the Indian market’s upward momentum, as domestic fundamentals remained stronger.

Rupee, rates and commodities

The rupee strengthened to 92.22 per dollar, a 0.73% decline from the previous close, marking an eight‑month high. A firmer rupee reduces the cost of dollar‑denominated debt for Indian corporates and improves the purchasing power of import‑dependent sectors, which in turn supports earnings outlooks. The currency’s appreciation also eases inflationary pressures, giving the Reserve Bank of India room to maintain a neutral stance on rates, a factor that underpins the equity rally seen today.

U.S. 10‑year Treasury yields slipped 0.28% to 4.90%, while Brent crude fell 0.30% to $88.51 and gold dropped 0.82% to $3,561.60. The lower yield environment eases financing costs globally, benefiting Indian exporters and infrastructure firms. Meanwhile, the dip in oil prices curtails input cost pressures for energy‑intensive industries, and the retreat in gold signals a shift away from safe‑haven assets toward riskier equities. Bitcoin also fell 1.54% to $58,822.84, underscoring a broader risk‑on tilt that favours equity markets over crypto.

Looking ahead to Friday, investors will watch for any fresh data on U.S. inflation and Fed commentary that could move yields, as well as domestic corporate earnings updates that may validate the current optimism. A continued firm rupee and stable commodity prices would likely sustain the rally, while any surprise weakening in the dollar or a spike in the India VIX could introduce volatility. Overall, the macro backdrop remains supportive, but the market remains vigilant for any shift in global risk sentiment that could alter the trajectory.
