What happened

India

The Nifty 50 closed at 25,884.50, up 2.11% on Thursday, while the Sensex finished at 84,328.59, up 2.30%. Gains were anchored by a 4.82% jump in the Nifty IT index, which lifted the broader market. Pharma (+2.84%) and FMCG (+2.82%) also posted solid advances, and the realty index rose 3.29%, suggesting renewed confidence in consumer‑driven segments. Energy added 0.61% and auto edged up 0.47%, offsetting modest drags from financials (-0.10%) and PSU banks (-0.31%). The India VIX fell 4.89% to 16.84, indicating a calmer risk environment after the rally.

Banking stocks contributed to the upside despite a slight pullback in the broader financials index, which slipped 0.10% to 30,649.45. The Nifty Bank outperformed, climbing 1.52% to 63,535.30, reflecting better earnings expectations and a stable credit outlook. PSU banks fell 0.31% to 9,529.90, hinting at lingering concerns over public‑sector balance sheets. The divergence suggests that private‑bank exposure and higher‑yielding loan books are attracting capital, while investors remain cautious on state‑run lenders amid policy uncertainty.

Commodities‑linked sectors showed a mixed picture. The metal index dropped 0.89% to 11,713.50, pressured by weaker global steel demand and a modest rise in Brent crude to $92.26 per barrel. Conversely, the energy index rose 0.61% to 36,880.30, buoyed by higher oil prices and expectations of tighter supply following recent Middle‑East tensions. The contrast underscores how rising crude can hurt metal producers while supporting energy‑focused equities, a dynamic that may influence portfolio allocations ahead of the Indian session.

Overall market breadth was strong, with six of eight major sector indices posting gains. The decline in the India VIX to 16.84 points signals reduced fear, and the modest appreciation in the rupee at 92.44 per dollar suggests no immediate currency stress. With the Nifty and Sensex both up over 2% and volatility easing, the market appears positioned for a positive open, though investors will watch upcoming domestic data and earnings releases for any shift in sentiment.

Global markets

US equity markets closed mixed on Wednesday: the S&P 500 fell 0.25% to 7,182.78, the Nasdaq slipped 0.62% to 23,585.80, while the Dow Jones rose 0.33% to 50,182.95. The split reflects lingering concerns over Fed policy and AI‑related stock valuations, but the modest Dow gain hints at resilience in industrials. European equities offered a brighter note, with the FTSE 100 up 1.75% to 10,609.30 on Thursday, driven by stronger earnings expectations and a firmer pound.

In Asia, the Nikkei 225 is trading live up 0.96% at 58,052.34, and the Hang Seng is up 0.52% at 22,341.52. Both indices are buoyed by a combination of weaker yen expectations and improved risk appetite after the European rally. The Asian gains reinforce a regional trend of modest optimism, which often translates into supportive flows for Indian equities, especially in export‑sensitive and technology‑heavy stocks.

The global mix of a cautious US, a bullish Europe, and rising Asian markets creates a nuanced backdrop for India. The US dip tempers risk appetite, but the European rally and Asian strength provide enough upside bias to sustain the Indian rally seen on Thursday. Investors will likely weigh the US earnings calendar and Fed commentary against the positive European momentum when positioning for the Indian open.

Rupee, rates and commodities

The rupee held steady at 92.44 per dollar, unchanged from the previous close, while the US 10‑year Treasury yield fell 1.14% to 4.85%. A lower yield environment typically supports Indian bond prices and can lower financing costs for corporates, adding a subtle boost to equity valuations. The stable rupee also reduces import‑cost pressures, helping margin stability for import‑dependent sectors.

Brent crude rose 1.79% to $92.26 per barrel, reflecting heightened geopolitical risk in the Middle East. Higher oil prices feed into domestic inflation expectations and lift energy‑related stocks, as seen in the 0.61% rise in the Indian energy index. However, the same price pressure can erode consumer spending, potentially weighing on auto and FMCG demand if the trend persists.

Gold advanced 2.21% to $3,643.50 per ounce, while Bitcoin climbed 1.59% to $61,455.74. The gold rally underscores a safe‑haven tilt amid global uncertainty, which may attract risk‑averse investors away from equities. Crypto’s modest rise is largely a peripheral factor for Indian markets but signals continued appetite for alternative assets. Together, these movements suggest a balanced risk sentiment, with investors weighing safety against growth opportunities.
