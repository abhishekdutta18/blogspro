What happened

India

Indian equities closed sharply lower on Monday, with the Nifty 50 falling 1.75% to 25,349.65 and the Sensex dropping 1.77% to 82,435.99. The decline was driven by a broad-based sell-off, particularly in the financial sector, where the Nifty Bank index tumbled 3.17% to 62,585.35. This sharp drop in banking stocks suggests a significant de-risking move by institutional investors, likely reflecting concerns over global liquidity and domestic valuation pressures.

Sectoral performance was mixed, with the Nifty IT index bucking the trend to rise 1.69% to 31,850.80, providing a narrow pocket of strength. In contrast, the Metal sector led the losses, falling 3.68% to 11,819.25, followed by PSU Banks at -3.25% and Financials at -2.31%. The Energy sector also declined by 1.78%, while defensive names like Realty managed a marginal 0.31% gain. This divergence highlights a rotation out of cyclical and high-beta stocks into more defensive or tech-oriented positions.

Volatility in the Indian market increased, with the India VIX rising 2.18% to 17.70. This spike in the fear gauge indicates that market participants are pricing in higher uncertainty for the coming sessions. The combination of a sharp index decline and rising volatility suggests that the current correction is not merely a technical adjustment but a fundamental reassessment of risk, particularly in sectors that have seen significant run-ups in recent months.

The sell-off in financials and metals is likely to persist into the next session, given the global context of a tech-led correction. Indian investors are closely watching how the US market's reaction to AI valuations translates into domestic sentiment. With the Nifty 50 closing below the 25,500 mark, technical support levels will be critical in determining whether this is a temporary dip or the start of a more prolonged consolidation phase for the broader market.

Global markets

The US market is currently trading lower, with the Nasdaq leading the decline at -2.31% to 23,533.64, while the S&P 500 is down 1.05% at 7,160.83. The Dow Jones Industrial Average is the only major index in positive territory, up 0.16% at 49,673.32. This divergence is driven by a sharp reversal in the AI-driven rally, with Nvidia shares falling 6% after a 30% surge on news of Jensen Huang's return as CEO. The market is now reassessing the sustainability of the AI investment cycle.

The correction in tech stocks is further exacerbated by the performance of Anthropic, whose shares have crashed below their $83 IPO price to $79.42, down 31% from their peak. This move has spooked investors and renewed concerns about AI valuations, infrastructure costs, and the competitive landscape. The Nasdaq's 2.31% drop reflects a broader loss of confidence in high-growth tech names, which are typically the primary drivers of global equity returns in the current cycle.

In Europe, the FTSE 100 is trading slightly lower at -0.28% to 10,451.65, showing relative resilience compared to the US tech-heavy indices. In Asia, the Nikkei 225 closed up 3.91% on Tuesday, while the Hang Seng index fell 0.85%. The mixed performance in Asia suggests that regional markets are reacting differently to the global tech correction, with Japan's market potentially benefiting from a yen-related trade or domestic factors. For India, the US tech weakness is a significant headwind, as it typically leads to a risk-off sentiment in emerging markets.

Rupee, rates and commodities

The US dollar remained stable against the Indian rupee, with USD/INR closing at 92.90, unchanged from the previous session. This stability suggests that the rupee is not yet under significant pressure from the global risk-off sentiment, but it also indicates a lack of strong domestic demand for the currency. The flat rupee is a neutral factor for Indian markets, as it does not provide a tailwind for foreign inflows or a headwind from currency depreciation.

US 10-year Treasury yields are down 0.69% to 4.88%, reflecting a flight to safety in the bond market as equity investors de-risk. This decline in yields is a positive for global equity valuations, as it reduces the discount rate applied to future cash flows. However, the simultaneous drop in equities and yields suggests that the market is in a defensive mode, with investors prioritizing capital preservation over growth. For Indian bonds, this could lead to a slight outflow of foreign investment, but the impact is likely to be limited given the domestic focus of the Indian bond market.

Commodity prices are mixed, with Brent crude up 0.38% to $87.01, while gold has fallen 2.55% to $3,558.40. The rise in oil prices is a negative for India, as it is a net importer of crude, and could widen the current account deficit. The drop in gold prices is a positive for Indian consumers and jewelers, but it also reflects a broader risk-off sentiment, as investors are not flocking to the traditional safe haven. Bitcoin is down 2.73% to $59,538.12, indicating that even crypto assets are not immune to the current market correction. The overall macro picture suggests a cautious environment for Indian assets, with oil prices and global tech sentiment being the key variables to watch.

Our view

cautious Analyst board aligned (3 of 3 views)

Global equities closed mixed to lower on Tuesday as the Nasdaq slipped 2.31% and the S&P 500 fell 1.05%, reflecting a sharp fade in the AI-driven rally that had underpinned risk appetite through much of the prior session. The Dow Jones managed a marginal gain of 0.16%, while the FTSE 100 declined 0.28% and the Nikkei 225 rose 3.91% in its closed Tuesday session, indicating uneven sentiment across developed markets. In fixed income, the US 10-year yield slipped 0.69% to 4.88%, reinforcing a risk-off tone that extended into commodities and digital assets. Brent crude edged higher by 0.38% to 87.01 USD, yet gold plunged 2.55% to 3558.40 USD and Bitcoin dropped 2.73% to 59538.12 USD, confirming broad de-risking. The dollar index was flat against the rupee, with USD/INR closing at 92.90 INR and unchanged on Tuesday, leaving the rupee stable. These global headwinds directly inform the outlook for Indian markets, which closed the Monday 23 November session with the Nifty 50 down 1.75% at 25349.65 INR and the Sensex falling 1.77% to 82435.99 INR, while the Nifty Bank plunged 3.17% to 62585.35 INR and the India VIX spiked 2.18% to 17.70 INR, signalling elevated volatility ahead of tomorrow's open.

The domestic market breadth on Monday was deeply negative outside a narrow band of outperformers, with only Nifty IT advancing 1.69% to 31850.80 INR and Realty adding 0.31% to 941.85 INR while the broader NSE sectors capitulated. Financials dropped 2.31% to 30017.30 INR, PSU Bank fell 3.25% to 9559.65 INR, Auto declined 1.03% to 28490.70 INR, Pharma slipped 0.35% to 28288.85 INR, FMCG fell 0.46% to 48312.55 INR, Metal plunged 3.68% to 11819.25 INR, Energy dropped 1.78% to 36655.10 INR, and the Nifty 50 itself closed 1.75% lower. This dispersion underscores that the recent rally lacked conviction and that sectoral weakness is now concentrated in rate-sensitive and commodity-linked pockets. The house view remains cautious because the global risk-off environment, evidenced by the Nasdaq slide and the collapse in Anthropic shares below their IPO price of 83 USD after a record debut, is pressuring Indian equities through capital flow channels. With the India VIX elevated and banks leading the decline, the near-term setup favours defensive positioning rather than aggressive accumulation.

What would change our view

A sustained rebound in the Nasdaq above its prior levels and a clear break in the India VIX below 17.00 INR would invalidate the cautious house stance by proving that global risk appetite has recovered and domestic volatility has normalised, thereby supporting a rotation back into Financials and Energy.

If Brent crude retreats sharply from 87.01 USD and the US 10-year yield resumes its decline beyond the current 4.88% level, financing costs could ease sufficiently to lift cyclical sectors, undermining the underweight bias on rate-sensitive pockets like Metal and PSU Bank.

Geopolitical de-escalation around Ukraine and Venezuela, or a rapid resolution of the debt overhang at 237.5 billion USD, could restore confidence in risk assets and trigger a broad equity rally that contradicts the current cautious positioning across the board.
