What happened

India

The Nifty 50 closed at 25819.25 INR, up 1.85% on Wednesday, while the Sensex rose 1.82% to 83938.28 INR. This sharp rebound followed a period of volatility, with the index reclaiming key technical levels. The Nifty Bank index gained 1.79% to 63703.25 INR, indicating that financials provided a solid base for the broader market rally. The move was broad-based but led by defensive and consumption-heavy names rather than the high-beta tech stocks that have been under pressure globally.

Sectoral performance was mixed, with FMCG leading the charge at +3.79% and Pharma following at +2.38%. Financials also performed well, rising 2.21%, while Auto added 1.16%. In contrast, the Metal sector lagged significantly, falling 2.04%, likely due to global commodity price pressures and a shift in investor sentiment away from cyclical industrial plays. Energy and Realty remained flat, suggesting that the rally was concentrated in specific defensive and financial pockets rather than a uniform market-wide surge.

The India VIX remained unchanged at 17.70, indicating that while the market moved sharply, the underlying fear gauge did not spike. This suggests that the rally was driven by a calculated shift in positioning rather than a panic-driven short cover. The stability in volatility levels is a positive sign for the sustainability of the move, as it implies that investors are not overly anxious about the global macro environment, despite the recent turbulence in US tech stocks.

The primary driver for the Indian market was a defensive rotation, with investors seeking safety in FMCG and Pharma amid global uncertainty. The strength in private banks and financials suggests that domestic liquidity and earnings expectations remain robust. However, the underperformance of metals and the flat performance of energy sectors highlight that the rally is not yet a full-blown risk-on event. The market is currently in a mode of selective participation, favoring sectors with strong domestic demand and predictable cash flows.

The IT sector, a key component of the Nifty 50, rose 1.12% to 32207.55 INR, which is a notable gain given the global AI rout. This relative strength suggests that Indian IT stocks are being viewed as a less risky alternative to their US counterparts, or that the domestic market is decoupling from the global tech narrative. The ability of IT to post a double-digit percentage gain in a day of global tech weakness is a significant positive for the sector's near-term outlook.

News that matters

  1. Trump unveils new tariffs over excess industrial capacity, undeterred by midterms defeat

    Reuters

    Trump unveiled new tariffs over excess industrial capacity, undeterred by midterms defeat, a move that directly challenges the premise of free trade and global supply chains. For Indian investors, this raises the prospect of retaliatory barriers on exports such as pharmaceuticals, textiles, and engineering goods, while also threatening the cost structure of domestic import-dependent industries. The narrative shift from deregulation to protectionism undermines the multi-year bull case for global manufacturing dispersion and could force Indian corporates to reassess overseas capex plans. Sectors most exposed include capital goods, autos, and metals, which rely on imported components and face input cost volatility. The story matters because it signals that geopolitical risk premiums are likely to remain elevated, compressing equity valuations for export-oriented and cyclical names. What would change the picture is clarity on which countries and product categories are targeted, and whether India receives explicit exemptions. Until those details emerge, the market must price in a higher baseline for trade friction and a slower pace of global demand growth.

    Reuters

    The AI stock rout deepened but damage remained limited by an afternoon bounce, indicating that sell-the-news sentiment in technology shares is still incomplete rather than terminal. For Indian markets, this matters because domestic IT indices such as Nifty IT, which closed up 1.12 percent on Wednesday, remain tethered to global software spending and enterprise capex cycles. The afternoon recovery suggests that bargain-hunting is capping losses, yet the underlying risk is that corporate budgets for artificial intelligence infrastructure could be trimmed if valuations stay stretched. Sectors affected include information technology, telecom services, and capital goods tied to data center build-outs. The narrative shifted from unquestioned AI capex expansion to a more sceptical view of near-term profitability. What would change the picture is confirmation of sustained order books from hyperscalers and clearer guidance on margin compression. Until then, Indian IT equities trade on a valuation discount to global peers but carry elevated execution risk if the afternoon bounce fails to hold.
