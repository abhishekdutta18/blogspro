What happened

India

The Nifty 50 closed at 25,607.20, down 0.82%, and the Sensex fell 0.71% to 83,339.26 on Friday. Bank Nifty slipped 1.04% to 63,040.90, while the IT index rose 1.71% to 32,758.10. India VIX dropped 6.37% to 16.55, signalling reduced fear. Breadth was mixed: Financials fell 1.21%, PSU Bank down 1.08%, Auto down 2.16%, Pharma flat at +0.04%, FMCG down 1.86%, Metal up 2.59%, Energy up 0.72% and Realty up 1.80%. The sell‑off was anchored by higher US Treasury yields and a stronger dollar, which pressured funding costs, while metal and IT outperformed on China‑linked demand and earnings optimism.

Bank‑focused indices led the decline, with Nifty Bank down 1.04% and PSU Bank losing 1.08%. Higher US 10‑year yields at 4.94% raised concerns about funding costs for Indian lenders, especially as the rupee held at 92.98 per dollar, a modest 0.59% strengthening. Mehul Kothari’s cautionary note on the ongoing correction resonated, prompting investors to trim exposure in financially sensitive stocks. Meanwhile, the auto sector’s 2.16% drop reflected weaker consumer sentiment, whereas the modest 0.72% rise in Energy mirrored Brent’s slight gain. The market narrative shifted from a broad correction to a more selective, risk‑aware stance ahead of the RBI meeting.

The IT sector’s 1.71% rise was the only bright spot, driven by strong earnings guidance from several large software houses. Metal’s 2.59% gain was underpinned by China’s supportive industrial policy, while Realty’s 1.80% advance hinted at renewed confidence in housing demand. Despite the overall downside, the reduced VIX suggested that panic was receding. Investors appear to be moving from a defensive posture to a more nuanced approach, seeking pockets of strength while remaining wary of macro headwinds. Upcoming events – the RBI policy review, corporate earnings releases, and global data – will likely dictate whether the market can sustain this selective buying.

Going into the Indian open, the bias remains defensive. The rupee’s modest strength at 92.98 and the rise in US yields suggest caution on the funding front, while the IT and metal sectors offer relative value. Traders should watch the Nifty Bank and Financials for further weakness, consider short positions if the sell‑off deepens, and look to overweight IT, Metal and Realty on the back of sector‑specific catalysts. Key events include the RBI’s monetary policy decision, US jobs data, and European budget talks, all of which could swing sentiment. A disciplined, data‑driven stance that respects the prevailing risk‑off tone will be essential.

Global markets

US equity markets closed sharply higher on Friday, with the S&P 500 up 1.60% to 7,297.49, the Nasdaq gaining 2.31% to 24,129.50 and the Dow Jones rising 1.18% to 50,774.94. In Europe, the FTSE 100 climbed 0.84% to 10,698.81, while Asian benchmarks also posted gains: the Nikkei 225 advanced 2.11% to 58,711.11 and Hong Kong’s Hang Seng jumped 3.32% to 22,694.77. The rally came despite a stronger dollar and higher US yields, suggesting that earnings optimism and sector‑specific tailwinds outweighed macro concerns in the short term.

The US equity surge could translate into fresh foreign inflows for Indian equities, but the accompanying rise in the 10‑year Treasury yield to 4.94% raises the cost of capital for Indian borrowers. European budget negotiations, highlighted by the EU’s €1.6 trillion plan, are likely to keep risk sentiment in check, especially if fiscal debates intensify. Meanwhile, the Asian upside, led by Japan’s Nikkei, supports a positive outlook for export‑oriented Indian firms, as a stronger global demand backdrop may offset domestic funding pressures.

Overall, global risk sentiment remains mixed. The dollar’s 0.59% rise against the rupee reflects broader safe‑haven demand, while gold’s 2.91% jump to $3,668.50 per ounce underscores lingering uncertainty. Brent’s modest 0.22% increase to $90.84 per barrel suggests limited shock to commodity markets. These dynamics imply that Indian investors must balance the upside from global equity strength against the downside from a stronger dollar, higher yields and persistent safe‑haven demand.

Rupee, rates and commodities

The rupee closed at 92.98 per dollar, a 0.59% appreciation that reflects a modest strengthening against a dollar that rose across the session. US 10‑year Treasury yields climbed 1.82% to 4.94%, tightening global financing conditions. Brent crude edged up 0.22% to $90.84 a barrel, while gold surged 2.91% to $3,668.50 per ounce, indicating safe‑haven buying. Bitcoin, trading live, slipped 1.05% to $59,859.28. The combination of a stronger dollar, higher yields and rising gold creates a headwind for risk assets, but the rupee’s resilience offers some cushion for import‑dependent sectors and may support domestic consumption.

Higher US yields tend to lift Indian government bond yields, raising borrowing costs for corporates and banks, which could pressure the banking sector’s margins. The rupee’s modest strength helps contain imported inflation, especially as oil prices remain relatively stable, but a further dollar rally could erode that benefit. Gold’s rally may divert capital from equities toward safe‑haven assets, while the stable oil price supports the Energy index’s modest 0.72% gain. Overall, macro variables are pointing toward a tighter financial environment, reinforcing the need for a defensive tilt in equity allocations.

Looking ahead, investors should monitor US jobs data and Fed commentary for clues on the trajectory of yields, as any further increase could intensify capital outflows from emerging markets. European fiscal negotiations will also shape risk appetite, while RBI policy decisions will determine domestic liquidity conditions. If the rupee weakens or yields climb sharply, defensive sectors such as Consumer Staples and Utilities may gain relative appeal. Conversely, a stable rupee and steady oil prices could keep Metal and IT sectors attractive, provided earnings remain robust. Maintaining a data‑driven, risk‑aware stance will be key to navigating the evolving macro backdrop.
