Indian equities retreated sharply on Monday as global tech stocks fell and financials slumped, while the rupee remained flat at 92.90 per dollar. Volatility rose and investors are likely to stay defensive ahead of key domestic and overseas data releases.

What happened

India

The Nifty 50 closed at 25,349.65, down 1.75% from the previous close, while the Sensex slipped to 82,435.99, a 1.77% decline. Bank Nifty fell the most, dropping 3.17% to 62,585.35, reflecting pressure on financials after the US tech sell‑off. The IT index bucked the trend, gaining 1.69% to 31,850.80, as domestic software exporters found some support from a stable rupee. The India VIX rose 2.18% to 17.70, signalling heightened nervousness among market participants. The overall move was anchored by a combination of global risk aversion and weaker domestic financial sector sentiment.

Sector breadth was narrow, with most major groups in the red. Financials fell 2.31% to 30,017.30 and PSU banks dropped 3.25% to 9,559.65, dragging the broader market lower. Metals suffered the steepest decline, down 3.68% to 11,819.25, while energy slipped 1.78% to 36,655.10. Auto and pharma were modestly down, at 1.03% and 0.35% respectively, and FMCG fell 0.46% to 48,312.55. Only the realty index managed a small gain of 0.31% to 941.85, highlighting the lack of defensive rotation into consumer staples or utilities.

The India VIX climbed to 17.70, up 2.18% on the day, reflecting a rise in market anxiety after the Nvidia episode and renewed speculation on Fed policy. Higher volatility was also fed by the widening of spreads in the banking sector and the metal sell‑off, which together amplified risk premia. Traders appeared to be pricing in a potential continuation of global risk aversion, especially as the US equity rally stalled and European markets showed only marginal resilience. The elevated VIX suggests that short‑term directional bets will be priced at a premium, and that any surprise on the macro front could trigger sharper moves.

Going into the Indian open, the bias remains defensive. With the rupee steady at 92.90 and US yields easing, capital flows may stay muted unless domestic data or RBI policy cues shift sentiment. Investors are likely to favour high‑quality banks that can weather credit stress, while staying cautious on high‑beta tech and metal stocks that have already sold off. Key events to watch include the RBI’s upcoming monetary policy statement, US Fed minutes for clues on rate trajectory, and corporate earnings from the banking and IT sectors. A breakout in the VIX or a surprise move in US yields could quickly alter positioning.

Global markets

In the United States, the S&P 500 closed at 7,201.09, down 0.50%, while the Nasdaq slipped 1.48% to 23,733.53, reflecting the fading of the Nvidia‑driven rally and renewed concerns over Fed tightening. The Dow Jones, however, managed a 0.85% rise to 50,018.70, buoyed by defensive industrials and energy stocks. The tech pullback reduced appetite for growth‑oriented Indian equities, particularly those with exposure to US‑linked software and services, and reinforced a risk‑off tone that filtered into the Indian market.

European markets showed modest weakness, with the FTSE 100 ending at 10,467.07, down 0.13%. The limited decline suggested that European investors were digesting the US tech correction without triggering a broader sell‑off. Eurozone data released earlier in the week hinted at a slowdown in manufacturing, adding to the cautious backdrop. For Indian exporters, a softer European equity environment may translate into weaker demand for commodities and a marginal impact on foreign‑currency earnings, reinforcing the need for hedging.

Asian markets diverged sharply. Japan’s Nikkei 225 surged 3.91% to 58,644.63, driven by a rebound in export‑oriented manufacturers and a weaker yen. In contrast, Hong Kong’s Hang Seng fell 0.85% to 22,219.65, reflecting regional risk aversion and concerns over Chinese policy. The Japanese rally offered a glimpse of upside for Indian firms with exposure to Asian supply chains, while the Hang Seng weakness underscored lingering uncertainty in the broader Asian equity space. Overall, the mixed Asian performance adds a layer of complexity to the risk calculus for Indian investors.

Rupee, rates and commodities

The rupee held at 92.90 per dollar, unchanged from the previous close, indicating a balance between modest dollar weakness and domestic capital flows. A stable exchange rate helps contain import‑related cost pressures for Indian corporates, especially those reliant on crude oil and raw material imports. However, the lack of rupee appreciation also means that any further dollar strength could quickly erode margins, keeping the currency outlook cautious.

US Treasury yields eased, with the 10‑year benchmark falling 0.28% to 4.90%, suggesting that inflation expectations may be moderating after the Fed’s recent rate hikes. Brent crude slipped 0.42% to $88.41 per barrel, providing some relief to the Indian import bill, while gold retreated 0.36% to $3,548.90 per ounce, reflecting reduced safe‑haven demand. Bitcoin also fell 2.41% to $59,737.30, underscoring the broader risk‑off sentiment across asset classes.

The macro backdrop points to a modestly supportive environment for Indian equities. Lower US yields can improve global equity valuations, while stable rupee and softer oil prices reduce cost pressures on Indian companies. The dip in gold suggests that investors are less inclined toward safe‑haven assets, potentially freeing capital for riskier bets. Nonetheless, the lingering volatility in crypto and the modest pullback in commodities mean that any adverse shock could quickly reverse the current equilibrium, so a cautious stance remains prudent.

What would change our view

A sharp reversal in global risk appetite, such as a sustained rally in the Nasdaq and a break above recent highs in the S&P 500 during the Tuesday session, would invalidate the cautious house stance by validating the fading AI rally narrative and reducing India VIX pressure ahead of the Indian open. If US 10-year yields resume a steeper decline rather than merely edging lower, the resulting search for yield could rotate capital back into Indian financials and cyclicals, undermining the underweight bias. Furthermore, any resolution of the Nvidia export-control allegations or positive resolution of the $237.5 billion Venezuela debt restructuring could act as a non-India catalyst that lifts global equity benchmarks and forces a repricing of Indian beta.
