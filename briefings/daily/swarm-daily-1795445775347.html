The Nifty 50 and Sensex closed down more than 1.7% on Monday, led by a 3.2% fall in Bank Nifty, while US equity indices surged on renewed AI enthusiasm. A weaker rupee, higher US yields and falling oil prices add pressure, leaving investors cautious ahead of the Indian open.

What happened

India

The Nifty 50 finished at 25,349.65, down 1.75% from the previous close, while the Sensex slipped to 82,435.99, a 1.77% decline. Bank Nifty bore the brunt, falling 3.17% to 62,585.35, whereas the IT index bucked the trend, rising 1.69% to 31,850.80. Volatility spiked, with India VIX climbing 2.18% to 17.70, signalling heightened nervousness among market participants after a week of sharp corrections.

Sector breadth was broadly negative. Financials dropped 2.31% to 30,017.30 and PSU banks fell 3.25% to 9,559.65, reflecting lingering concerns over credit quality and recent debt auctions. Metals slumped 3.68% to 11,819.25 and energy slipped 1.78% to 36,655.10, while autos and FMCG were modestly down at 1.03% and 0.46% respectively. Pharma edged lower by 0.35% and only the realty index managed a small gain of 0.31%, underscoring a risk‑off tilt across most of the market.

The rise in India VIX to 17.70 points mirrored the sell‑off in banks and heavy‑weight sectors, while the rupee weakened to 92.90 per dollar, up 0.26% on the day. Despite a headline report of $12.5 billion of FPI inflows into India, the market remained under pressure as a recent state bond auction pushed yields higher and corporate debt supply concerns lingered. The NSEL settlement of Rs 1,950 crore provided a brief positive note, but it was insufficient to offset the broader risk aversion.

Going into the Indian open, positioning should remain defensive. Investors may look to quality banks with stronger balance sheets, while the IT sector could benefit from the global AI rally if earnings support the hype. Heavy exposure to metals, energy and auto should be trimmed given the sector weakness and higher financing costs. Keep an eye on RBI policy cues, upcoming corporate news such as Adani Airport’s $300 million raise, and any further debt supply surprises that could amplify yield pressure.

Global markets

US equity markets are trading sharply higher, with the S&P 500 up 4.29% at 7,206.87, the Nasdaq gaining 6.04% to 23,983.77, and the Dow Jones rising 2.51% to 49,356.71. The surge follows Nvidia’s CEO reinstatement, which reignited AI‑related buying, but analysts caution that valuations remain stretched and the rally may be short‑lived. For Indian investors, the upside in US tech could translate into renewed FPI interest, yet domestic banking stress tempers the net inflow potential.

In Europe, the FTSE 100 posted a modest 0.82% gain to 10,431.41, suggesting a tentative risk‑on mood on the continent. In contrast, Asian equity benchmarks were mixed: the Nikkei 225 closed down 5.06% on Friday at 56,436.65, reflecting weak Chinese industrial data and a steel sector slowdown, while Hong Kong’s Hang Seng slipped 0.12% to 22,383.74. The Asian weakness may dampen demand for Indian metal exports and keep foreign investors cautious about emerging‑market exposure.

The global narrative is one of a sharp US rally driven by AI hype, modest European optimism, and continued Asian softness. While US gains could lift global risk appetite, the uneven performance across regions means capital flows into India will likely be selective, favoring sectors with clear earnings upside. Investors should monitor whether the AI‑driven rally sustains or gives way to profit‑taking, as that will shape the tone of cross‑border fund movements in the coming days.

Rupee, rates and commodities

The rupee weakened to 92.90 per dollar, up 0.26% from the prior close, indicating a modest loss of ground against the greenback. At the same time, the US 10‑year Treasury yield rose 0.90% to 4.96%, reflecting tighter global monetary conditions. Higher US yields tend to lift Indian sovereign yields, pressuring domestic borrowing costs and potentially attracting yield‑seeking foreign capital, but the concurrent rupee depreciation adds import‑price pressure, especially for oil‑intensive sectors.

Commodities moved in opposite directions: Brent crude fell 2.01% to $88.14 a barrel, easing inflation concerns for India’s import‑dependent economy, while gold rose 4.39% to $3,669.80, underscoring safe‑haven demand amid market volatility. Bitcoin also climbed 2.85% to $62,908.25, though crypto exposure remains marginal for most Indian investors. The drop in oil prices could support consumer spending, yet the gold rally may keep the India VIX elevated as investors seek protection.

The transmission of these macro variables to Indian assets is mixed. A weaker rupee and higher global yields can compress equity valuations, particularly for import‑sensitive stocks like autos and pharma, while lower oil prices may boost consumption‑driven sectors. The rise in gold prices aligns with the higher VIX, suggesting that risk aversion will persist. Consequently, a cautious stance—favoring defensive equities, monitoring RBI policy signals, and watching the upcoming state bond auction—appears prudent for the day ahead.
